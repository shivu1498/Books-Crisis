Market Impact

How markets moved before and after every event since share trading began in Amsterdam in 1602, plus detailed studies of the biggest crashes.

Each chart shows the US stock market (S&P composite, Robert Shiller’s monthly series, extended with FRED data) from two years before to two years after the event year, which is shaded. Values are monthly averages, so one-day crashes look smaller than they were. It is the longest continuous market series available, so it also stands in for events outside the US. It starts in 1871: earlier events have no comparable index and are marked as such, except 1720, which has its own study.

Charts join key closing levels from standard published index histories. They are not daily data, and levels marked approx. are rounded. Headlines are short summaries of the news of the time, not verbatim newspaper headlines.

Method & Caveats

How to read the register before drawing conclusions from it.

Severity scale

  • 0No significant recorded event, or an institutional milestone with no loss event.
  • 1Local or minor.
  • 2Notable but contained within one institution, city or court.
  • 3Major national or single-market event.
  • 4Major multi-market or cross-border crisis.
  • 5Systemic; reshaped the financial system of its era.

Do not read the trend naively

Event frequency rises from about 15% of years before 1300 to 100% of years since 1990. Most of that slope is record survival and market coverage, not risk. The register measures what was written down at least as much as what occurred.

Two things are real: from roughly 1600 there are tradeable assets, so the bubble becomes possible; and the share of severity 4–5 events that spread across borders rises sharply after 1850 and again after 1971.

The patterns that hold

Sovereign concentration. A lender with concentrated exposure to a borrower who cannot be compelled to pay: the Riccardi (1294), the Bardi and Peruzzi (1343–46), the Fuggers (1557), Barings (1890), the 1982 Mexico syndicate.

Collateral you did not verify. Salad oil (1963), Bre-X (1997), Madoff (2008), Wirecard (2020), Tricolor and First Brands (2025).

Debt relief by fiat. Japan’s tokusei edicts (1297, 1441) and the York bond-burning (1190): cancelling debt destroys the credit supply it was meant to restore.

Caveats

Quiet years. A zero-severity year means the surviving record is silent, not that the year was stable. Severe before 1300, serious before 1500, mild after 1800.

Geographic bias. Coverage of Europe is denser than the Islamic world, China, India or Japan, and the gap widens further back.

Figures. Pre-20th-century amounts are not inflation-adjusted. Post-2024 figures are as reported and may be revised.

Excluded by design. Ordinary recessions, routine bear markets and normal enforcement actions.
